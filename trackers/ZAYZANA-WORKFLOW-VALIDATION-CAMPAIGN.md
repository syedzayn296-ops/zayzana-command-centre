# Zayzana Workflow Validation Campaign

## Purpose
Validate whether South African SMEs will pay Zayzana Tech to solve one painful, frequent, manual operational workflow.

## Commercial hypothesis
Businesses already use WhatsApp, email, spreadsheets, CRMs, accounting systems and other software. The gap is often the workflow between those systems: enquiry/order/request -> qualification -> action -> follow-up -> result.

## Offer
Do not sell a generic CRM, chatbot, AI agent or ERP.

Sell a discovery-led workflow pilot:
1. Discover one operational bottleneck.
2. Map the current process.
3. Identify measurable waste, delay, missed follow-up or poor visibility.
4. Design the smallest useful workflow.
5. Pilot it for 14-30 days.
6. Measure before/after.
7. Decide whether to improve, stop or roll out.

## Campaign target
24 prospects in three batches of eight.

Batch 1 is the active validation cohort. Do not automatically release Batch 2 until Batch 1 responses are reviewed.

## Validation questions
1. What repetitive process is still handled manually?
2. What comes in and through which channel?
3. Who handles it?
4. Where is information copied or re-entered?
5. Where does it get delayed or lost?
6. How often does the problem happen?
7. What does it cost in time, money, errors or missed opportunities?
8. What systems are currently used?
9. Who owns the problem?
10. Who can approve a pilot?
11. What outcome would make a solution worth paying for?
12. What would they realistically pay for a small pilot?

## Response codes
R0 = no response
R1 = rejection
R2 = interested but no problem identified
R3 = real problem identified
R4 = discovery conversation
R5 = qualified commercial opportunity
R6 = pilot discussion
R7 = paid pilot

## Validation thresholds
Minimum useful signal:
- 5+ prospects identify a real operational problem.

Strong signal:
- 8+ identify problems.
- 3+ agree to discovery conversations.

Very strong:
- 2+ discuss a specific pilot.

Commercial validation:
- At least 1 prospect pays for a pilot.

## What counts as evidence
"Sounds interesting" is not validation.
"Yes, this happens every day and costs us X" is strong pain evidence.
"We would pay if you could achieve Y" is a budget signal.
"Let's test it in one process/store/team" is pilot evidence.
Payment is the strongest validation.

## Positioning
Competitor framing:
- Avoid "we automate WhatsApp."
- Avoid "AI automation platform."
- Avoid "another CRM."

Zayzana framing:
"We find one repetitive business process that is costing you time, money or visibility, and build a small workflow around it."

## Pilot commercial bands
These are proposed Zayzana test prices, not market claims:
- Small SME: R3,500-R7,500 once-off pilot
- Growing SME: R7,500-R15,000
- Larger/multi-site: R15,000-R30,000+ depending on scope
- Ongoing support/automation: R750-R3,000+/month depending on infrastructure and value

Do not quote these blindly. First qualify the problem and value.

## Decision rule
Market -> problem -> offer -> pilot -> cash.

Do not code a generic platform before a paid workflow is identified.
